Decision Evidence Review: Eight Worked Record Checks

A decision file should answer a specific question, not merely look complete. This guide uses eight independent fictional packets to show how a proposed term, a file count, an estimated benefit or a growing balance can support a narrower conclusion than its label suggests. Each example has a different record problem and a concrete next request.

These are original website teaching exercises by Robertson Books, not manuscript excerpts, verified book summaries, client cases or professional conclusions about real transactions. A01 through W01 are separate scenarios. Do not combine their figures into one business or portfolio. The linked sources provide limited background; the examples and arithmetic are original to this guide.

For each packet, write down the question, the supplied record, what the record establishes, the missing evidence and who would review the next response. The download has an empty observed_review field for your own reading notes. A blank records no outcome; it does not mean zero cost, no response or a completed review.

A01: A proposed price is not an accepted price

The fictional buyer memo proposes $650,000 for a business. A retained seller email asks for $680,000. Neither record contains the other party’s acceptance, and no executed agreement is supplied. The $30,000 difference can be calculated, but the packet does not show whether either side changed its position or whether there is an agreed transaction.

A useful finding quotes neither party as having accepted the other’s number. It identifies the two records separately and requests the current agreed terms, if any, together with the relevant version and parties. Do not fill missing conditions with assumed customary provisions or treat a draft filename containing “final” as proof of agreement.

The SBA’s buying-a-business guidance discusses investigation and review of documents such as contracts, financial statements and tax returns with appropriate professional help. That background explains why the underlying documents matter. It does not value this invented business or determine the effect of a particular agreement.

Next review question: Which current terms and acceptance records would address the proposed commitment?

D01: Twelve filenames do not establish twelve usable records

The fictional folder index contains twelve named files. F01 is unreadable. F02 and F03 are readable but identify a different entity. F04 through F12 are readable and identify the intended entity. These groups do not overlap. None has a completed content-review entry.

Nine files pass the two supplied metadata checks: readable and matching entity. That is twelve minus one minus two. It does not mean nine reviews were completed or that the file covers every necessary issue. The twelve-row inventory CSV preserves the individual flags so you can reproduce the count without treating a folder total as evidence of completeness.

The next request distinguishes three problems: obtain a usable F01, resolve the subject mismatch in F02 and F03, and assign content review for the remaining records. The inventory cannot determine whether a document is accurate, current or sufficient for a real transaction. Its blank content_review fields are deliberately unknown, not failed reviews.

Next review question: Which usability, subject and content-review gaps require separate follow-up?

T01: An estimated benefit is not a filed result

The invented proposal headline estimates an $18,000 prospective tax reduction. The packet contains no calculation, source transaction records, preparer review or filing evidence. A planning label on the cover does not establish an allowable deduction, a refund, a completed election or an amount recovered from a prior return.

A precise review records the figure as an unsupported proposal estimate within this exercise. It asks which entity and period the estimate concerns, which calculation and supporting records produced it, and what action is actually proposed. Keep a prospective planning question separate from a claim that a return was filed or an earlier payment was recovered.

The IRS explains that supporting business documents underpin entries in the books and tax return. That recordkeeping background supports the request for source documents; it does not substantiate the invented $18,000 figure. No tax rate, eligibility finding, filing deadline or recommended tax treatment is supplied here.

Next review question: Which entity, period, calculations and underlying records support the exact proposed action?

E01: Interest responses are not purchase records

The fictional research log has fourteen responses: ten say interested and four say later. There are no checkout, payment or order records in the packet. Ten divided by fourteen is about 71.4% of responses expressing interest under the supplied labels. That fraction describes this small invented response set.

It is not a purchase-conversion rate, a count of paying customers or a forecast of demand. The absence of order records in this packet also does not prove there were zero orders elsewhere. A useful finding keeps response categories separate from transaction evidence and names the missing bridge.

Before drawing a sales conclusion, the review question would ask whether orders can be matched to the relevant response population and period. The exercise supplies neither selection method nor respondent identities. Do not treat fourteen responses as a representative market study, and do not add personal contact information to the public worksheet.

Next review question: Which transaction records and population definition would support a sales conclusion?

L01: Assignment and acknowledgement are different states

The invented handoff lists four tasks. Three have a named owner and one owner field is blank. Only two assigned tasks have acknowledgements attached. There are no completion records. The packet therefore shows three named assignments, two acknowledgements and one unassigned task, without proving any finished work.

Calling the whole handoff accepted would skip the third assigned person’s missing acknowledgement and the fourth task’s missing owner. Calling it complete would require another unsupported step. A review should preserve these states separately instead of turning a mostly populated table into a single green score.

The next request is specific: identify the missing owner, obtain the outstanding acknowledgement where needed, and define the evidence that would show each deliverable was completed. These are reading questions for the fictional packet, not messages already sent to real staff or a judgement about anyone’s performance.

Next review question: Which owner, acknowledgement and completion-evidence gaps remain?

R01: Risk management due diligence checklist for backup recovery

For a recovery-focused due diligence checklist, record the system and data in scope, the business function that depends on it, the date of the last test, the evidence showing what was recovered, any unresolved exceptions, and the person who reviewed the result. For each missing item, identify who will provide the evidence and when the buyer will review it.

The fictional checklist contains a row labeled restore complete, but the test date, result and reviewer fields are blank. The packet has no restoration log or retained output from a test. The label describes a step to be checked; it does not supply the missing test result.

A useful finding states that restoration evidence is absent from the packet. It does not conclude that a backup failed, that a real system is insecure or that someone skipped a test. Those would be additional claims. The open question concerns what evidence supports recovery of the intended data and functions.

The next request would identify the intended scope, a suitable test record and the person responsible for reviewing it. This exercise does not instruct anyone to restore or overwrite a production system. Keep confidential operational logs and credentials outside the teaching CSV; a non-sensitive reference is enough for your reading notes.

Next review question: Which scoped test record and reviewer finding would address the recovery claim?

C01: A reserved amount is part of the cash balance

The independent fictional allocation has $140,000 of starting cash, a $70,000 commitment and $25,000 of setup spending. Subtracting the two commitments leaves $45,000. Of that remaining balance, the worksheet designates $30,000 as reserved, leaving $15,000 unallocated.

A further proposed $20,000 commitment would exceed the unallocated amount by $5,000 if the reserve designation and all stated commitments stay in place. The reserve is part of the $45,000, not another account to add to it. The numbers do not establish what a real business should reserve or whether any proposed purchase is advisable.

The review asks whether the commitments, timing and reserve designation are still the intended inputs. No future revenue or alternative funding is supplied. If a later record changes an input, retain that change explicitly and recalculate; do not quietly use reserved cash while keeping the original reserve label.

Next review question: Which commitments, timing and reserve designation would need documented review before changing the allocation?

W01: Balance growth is not a measured investment return

The fictional opening balance is $250,000 and closing balance is $280,000. The packet also supplies $40,000 of contributions and explicitly no withdrawals. It does not supply contribution dates or a reconciliation of fees, taxes and investment activity. The balance increased by $30,000, or 12% of its opening amount.

After subtracting the supplied contributions, the unreconciled residual is negative $10,000: $280,000 minus $250,000 minus $40,000. Neither the 12% balance change nor that residual is a supported investment-return rate. The residual still needs attribution, and the timing needed for an appropriate return calculation is absent.

A useful next request asks for dated cash movements and a reconciliation of the balance changes. This packet does not show who caused a result, what future returns will be or whether a particular investment is suitable. Keep a reported balance, outside contributions and any calculated performance measure in separate fields.

Next review question: Which dated cash movements and activity reconciliation would support a performance calculation?

Record your own reading finding

Compare your notes with the stated interpretation, then identify the smallest next record that would change the conclusion. A useful request names the subject, period or version and the exact gap. It does not ask for every available file, and it does not assume that requesting a record means it has been received or reviewed.
