Read Numerical Range Endpoints Before an AI Book Summary

A range can lose its meaning in a very short summary. Between six and ten might refer to allowed values, observed values, selected scenarios or an uncertain estimate. At least six is different from more than six. A pair of endpoint numbers does not tell you whether every value between them is possible, whether the endpoints are included or whether any value has a probability attached. Before asking AI to explain a numerical passage, recover those details from the supplied material.

This guide develops an original reading workflow for preserving numerical bounds. Its six worked cases use fictional planning quantities and explicit teaching rules. No actual business book passage, manuscript, model response, client record, forecast or operating decision was inspected. There is no recommendation about an actual business threshold or financial choice. Exact checks can establish the stated arithmetic and membership relationships in these invented examples; they cannot validate an author's assumptions or make a real range reliable.

Name the quantity before interpreting its endpoints

Begin with a complete sentence naming the quantity that can vary. Include its unit, its population or object and the relevant period when those are supplied. A range of task counts is not a range of elapsed times. A range for one occurrence is not automatically a range for an average across many occurrences. If the passage does not identify the quantity, keep that question visible rather than treating the two numbers as a self contained result.

Give the range a role as well. Is it a declared constraint, a description of supplied observations or a set of hypothetical inputs? Those roles can use identical numbers while supporting different statements. A constraint says which values satisfy a written condition. An observed minimum and maximum summarize the observations actually supplied. Hypothetical endpoints describe choices made for a comparison. None of those roles alone establishes a probability distribution or a promise about a future observation.

A useful first instruction to an assistant is to return the quantity and the range's stated role before calculating anything. Require a source locator or supplied phrase for each. If the information is missing, ask for an unresolved label. This slows the tendency to treat any low and high number as an estimate with a familiar statistical interpretation. The assistant's task is to organize what the reader supplied, not to select a meaning that makes the paragraph sound more complete.

Preserve whether each endpoint belongs to the set

The University of Kentucky's interval notation lesson uses square brackets for included endpoints and parentheses for excluded endpoints. It distinguishes bounded intervals from unbounded ones and explains combining intervals with a union. That limited mathematical vocabulary is useful here. See https://www.ms.uky.edu/ma109/textbook/sec-intervalnotation.html. The examples below are independently authored and do not adopt the lesson's worked exercises, an actual book's notation or a business rule.

Fictional case A defines a real valued planning quantity x by 6 <= x < 10, written as [6,10). The value six belongs, seven and nine belong, and ten does not. A proposed summary saying from six through ten would add the excluded upper endpoint. A proposed summary saying greater than six would remove the included lower endpoint. The two endpoint decisions are independent details of the supplied set, and shortening the sentence must preserve both.

The difference may matter even when most tested values lie well inside the bounds. An explanation that gets seven and nine right can still get the set wrong at six or ten. Ask the assistant to test the two endpoints and one interior value against its own paraphrase. In this example, the endpoint checks are not an operational eligibility test. They simply make a changed inequality easier to spot before it becomes part of a reading note.

Do not assume that ordinary prose has settled an endpoint when the wording is ambiguous. Between six and ten may be clarified by surrounding notation, a definition or a table note. If that context has not been supplied, the reader should preserve the ambiguity. An assistant can list the possible interpretations without selecting one. That is more useful than returning a confident inequality whose endpoint convention came from the assistant rather than the source.

Distinguish an integer count from a continuous quantity

The interval's domain matters. A quantity explicitly restricted to whole counts can have a finite list of permitted values between two bounds. A real valued quantity can contain fractions and decimals between the same bounds. Those are different sets even if the endpoint numerals match. Record the domain before counting values or using an example as a test. A word such as units does not settle the issue unless the passage defines what can be divided.

Fictional case B restricts n to integers satisfying 6 <= n <= 10. The permitted values are six, seven, eight, nine and ten, a total of five. If the upper endpoint is excluded instead, there are four permitted integers: six through nine. The endpoint difference ten minus six is four in both descriptions. That difference measures the span between endpoint labels; it does not count the five included integers in the closed case.

An AI explanation can make a plausible error by equating span with count. Ask it to list the permitted values for this small teaching case and compare the list with its claimed total. This is a local check, not a general method for enumerating every real interval or every business constraint. If the domain is continuous, there are infinitely many real values between six and ten. Listing the five integers would silently replace the declared domain with a different one.

Keep counts and measurements distinct in the final note. If a passage reports a measured quantity with decimal values, do not remove the decimals merely because a neighboring table uses whole counts. Conversely, do not add fractional tasks to a quantity explicitly defined as a count of indivisible items. The task is to recover the source's quantity, not to impose a convenient domain for a calculation the assistant wants to perform.

Keep separate pieces separate when a range has a gap

Two allowed bands do not automatically form one continuous band. A summary can lose a gap by retaining only the smallest and largest number. That produces an enclosing interval, which may include values the original condition excluded. An enclosing interval can be useful for a particular purpose, but it must be labeled as an enclosure rather than substituted for the original set. The distinction is especially easy to miss when the gap is small relative to the full span.

Fictional case C permits the real intervals [0,2] and [5,7]. Their union includes one and six, but excludes three and four. The enclosing interval [0,7] includes three and four, so it is a larger set. The total length of the two permitted bands is four units, while the enclosing span has length seven. Those are geometric lengths in the declared teaching scale, not probabilities, observed coverage or a measure of business performance.

Ask the assistant to preserve the union symbol or an explicit or between the two conditions. If it replaces the pair with a single low to high phrase, have it test a value in the gap. Three provides a simple counterexample here: it belongs to the enclosure and not the union. This checks whether the paraphrase has changed the set without asking the assistant to judge why the gap exists or whether it is appropriate in an actual application.

A missing interval should also remain distinguishable from an excluded interval. If the source lists two bands and says that a third band was not reported, absence of information does not establish prohibition. This guide's fictional union is complete by definition; a real excerpt may not be. Ask whether the supplied material claims to describe the entire allowed set or only selected parts. Completeness is an assumption that needs its own source support.

Read and and or before combining two conditions

Two constraints can apply simultaneously or can be alternatives. Simultaneous constraints select values satisfying both, while alternatives select values satisfying at least one. A summary that drops the connecting word can change the result even if every numeral is copied correctly. Write each condition separately before combining them. The reader should be able to see which operation the wording requires and what the final set is meant to represent.

Fictional case D defines A as [2,8] and B as [5,11]. Values satisfying both lie in [5,8], the intersection. Values satisfying at least one lie in [2,11], the union. The value three satisfies A but not B; ten satisfies B but not A; six satisfies both. These three test values distinguish the two combinations. The example concerns declared real number sets, not an actual policy, authorization process or eligibility determination.

A reading note can say that the overlap is five through eight, including both endpoints, while the combined alternative band is two through eleven. That sentence states two different results and names their roles. It should not collapse them into one general range. If a passage uses and in ordinary prose without clear mathematical scope, return to the complete sentence. The word may connect descriptions rather than impose simultaneous numerical constraints, and a clipped excerpt can hide that distinction.

The same caution applies when conditions concern different quantities. A range for duration and a range for item count cannot be intersected as though their endpoint numbers occupied one shared scale. They may jointly describe an ordered pair of attributes, but that is a different object from one scalar interval. An assistant should identify the quantity attached to each condition before applying familiar set operations. Correct symbols do not repair a mismatch in what the symbols describe.

Do not turn a permitted interval into a uniform probability distribution

Knowing that a value lies between two bounds does not tell you how likely any part of that interval is. A midpoint is a number computed from endpoints, not automatically an expected value, median or most likely outcome. A reading summary should state the operation it has performed. If it averages two endpoint numbers, call the result their midpoint unless the supplied material establishes an additional interpretation. This prevents a simple arithmetic convenience from becoming an invented probability claim.

In case A, the midpoint of six and ten is eight. That remains true whether the upper endpoint is included or excluded. But the set [6,10) supplies no probability rule. It does not establish that eight is expected or that values below eight and above eight have equal probability. A later probability model could provide more information, but it would be an additional assumption. The original bounds alone do not supply it.

This is where a range reading task differs from reading an expected value. An expected value requires an identified probability model and quantity. A bound identifies a set or constraint and may carry no probability information at all. If a passage contains both, preserve both rather than replacing one with the other. The existing expected value guide addresses the probability weighted mean; this guide addresses the endpoint and set information that a mean can leave behind.

An assistant asked to make a passage easier to understand may add familiar phrases such as typically near the middle. Require it to locate the source support for that phrase. If none is present in the supplied material, remove the phrase or label it as an unadopted hypothetical assumption. A concise note can still be informative: the quantity lies within the stated bounds, endpoint membership is specified, and no probability distribution was supplied.

Preserve relationships when combining bounded quantities

Two quantities can each have a minimum and maximum while being linked to one another. Combining their separate bounds as if every pair of values were possible can produce an enclosure that is wider than the actual result. Before adding or subtracting endpoint values, ask what joint combinations the supplied material permits. The relationship may be a formula, a complete list of pairs or a condition that rules out some combinations. Separate ranges do not carry all of that information.

Fictional case E restricts x to the integers zero through four and defines y as four minus x. The complete pairs are (0,4), (1,3), (2,2), (3,1) and (4,0). Each separate quantity ranges from zero to four, but every permitted pair has x+y equal to four. Treating x and y as freely combinable would produce possible sums zero through eight. That wider set includes combinations such as (0,0) and (4,4) that the supplied relationship does not permit.

The correct conclusion is not that endpoint arithmetic is always wrong. It is that an enclosure based only on separate bounds can lose a dependency. The reader should say what information was used and whether the resulting bounds are exact for the declared joint set or merely contain it. Here the complete pair list makes the exact sum easy to inspect. No statistical correlation, actual data relationship or model behavior has been estimated.

Ask an assistant to keep the pair relationship in its working table. If it creates independent low and high scenarios, compare those scenarios with the supplied list before accepting them. A hypothetical pair absent from an explicitly complete list is not a discovered outcome. It is a changed assumption. This reading habit is useful whenever a summary combines quantities from one scenario into a best or worst case assembled from incompatible parts.

Distinguish an observed subset from bounds on the full quantity

A short excerpt can contain the smallest and largest values among the entries shown without describing the entire set. Calling those values the full range requires a completeness claim. Ask whether omitted entries exist and whether their values are known. A minimum among available records is not automatically a lower bound for unknown records; a maximum among available records is not automatically an upper bound. The scope of the records belongs in the statement.

Fictional case F supplies values four, nine and an unknown third value. Among the two known entries, the minimum is four and the maximum is nine. The full three entry minimum and maximum remain unresolved. If the third value were one, the full range would run from one to nine. If it were twelve, it would run from four to twelve. These are two explicitly hypothetical completions, not recovered observations or evidence that the missing value lies anywhere in particular.

Replacing the unknown with zero would give a different invented completion. Dropping the unknown would produce a summary of the known subset. Either operation can be stated clearly as a separate task, but neither establishes the original full range. Ask an assistant to return the known subset result and the unresolved full result in separate fields. That prevents the polished two number answer from hiding the reason a complete answer could not be calculated.

Keep the selection scope visible when comparing two excerpts. One may show a full table and the other only examples. Their displayed minima and maxima do not automatically describe comparable populations. A range can become wider simply because more values were included, but this guide does not infer that cause for any actual comparison. Recover the inclusion rules first, and reserve causal explanations for evidence beyond the endpoint arithmetic.

Build a prompt that keeps the reading task inspectable

Supply the exact passage, table notes and definitions you want analyzed, within your permitted reading context. Ask the assistant to identify the quantity, unit, domain, endpoint inclusions, connecting operations, completeness claim and any probability language. Require an unresolved response for missing fields. Then ask for a plain language paraphrase that preserves those fields. This sequence makes the explanation easier to check because each claimed detail has a place in the supplied material.

For a small numerical example, request a short list of test values: below a lower bound, at each endpoint, inside the interval and in any gap. Make clear that the tests are demonstrations of the supplied rule, not evidence about a real process. Review them yourself against the original condition. A test table can expose an inconsistency in a paraphrase, but passing a few chosen examples does not prove a general parser, model or application conforms to every possible rule.

Keep hypothetical completions in a separate section. If the unknown value is one or twelve in case F, those substitutions should remain labeled wherever reused. Do not let the assistant merge them into a single claimed observed range of one to twelve. That combined statement would report endpoints taken from different invented completions. A comparison of possibilities is useful precisely when it preserves which assumption produced each result.

Review the final summary for what it has added or removed

Read the assistant's proposed summary as a new document. Check that an included endpoint has not disappeared, an excluded endpoint has not entered, a gap has not been filled and an unknown has not become zero. Check that the quantity and domain still match. Then look for added claims about typicality, likelihood, reliability or desirability. Those words can carry assumptions that were absent from the numerical passage even when the copied endpoint numbers are correct.

Record what remains unresolved as part of the reading result. You might know the membership convention while lacking the population scope, or understand the two bands while not knowing why the gap exists. That is a useful map of the next reading question. A complete sounding paragraph is not the objective if it requires inventing missing context. The reader needs a faithful account of what the supplied range means and what further material would be needed to say more.

Questions about numerical ranges in AI assisted reading

Does between two numbers tell me whether the endpoints are included?

Not necessarily. Look for explicit inequalities, notation or a definition in the surrounding material. If that context was not supplied, keep the convention unresolved. The fictional half open interval case shows why both endpoints need separate attention. An assistant should not choose a convention merely because one makes the summary shorter or resembles another example.

Is the difference between endpoints the number of permitted integers?

Not in the closed integer example. Six through ten includes five integers, while ten minus six is four. Excluding ten leaves four integers. Count depends on the domain and endpoint membership, whereas endpoint subtraction measures the span. Neither result tells you how frequently any value occurs or whether a real rule uses those bounds.

Can I replace two separate bands with their overall minimum and maximum?

That creates an enclosure and can fill a gap. The fictional union [0,2] or [5,7] excludes three, while [0,7] includes it. Label an enclosure as an enclosure if that is the task. Do not present it as the original allowed set. A list of selected bands also needs a completeness statement before it can describe a full set.

Is the midpoint an expected value?

A midpoint is the arithmetic average of two endpoint numbers. An expected value requires probability information that an interval alone does not provide. Eight is the midpoint of six and ten, but the fictional set [6,10) supplies no distribution. Keep the midpoint label unless additional supplied material establishes a probability weighted interpretation.

Why can separate ranges give an overly broad range for a sum?

Separate bounds can omit a relationship between the quantities. In the fictional complete pair list, x and y each span zero through four, but y equals four minus x, so every permitted sum is four. Freely combining the separate endpoints adds pairs the relationship excluded. Preserve the joint rule before describing the exact possible sums.

What do these checks establish about an AI summary?

They establish the finite arithmetic and membership relationships in the authored cases, not the accuracy of an actual model response. No model was run and no business book passage was interpreted here. A reader can use the cases to review a supplied paraphrase, while the validity of real definitions, observations and decisions remains a separate question.
