Separate Causal Claims Before Asking AI for a Book Summary

A reading note can change an argument with one verb. A book passage may describe two things occurring together, while a hurried summary says that one caused the other. An AI assistant can make that change sound natural because causal stories are easy to read. Before accepting the summary, identify the relationship actually stated, the comparison offered as evidence and the additional assumptions needed for an explanation about cause and effect.

This guide is a practical reading workflow using original fictional notes, small authored tables and proposed wording. None is a quotation from a published business book, an inspected manuscript, a chapter-method attribution or an observed model response. The arithmetic checks establish differences, proportions and field relationships in six supplied examples. They do not estimate causal effects, validate research findings or evaluate an AI system. Keep the source passage, your interpretation and a possible application distinct throughout the task.

Find the claim before judging its explanation

Start by locating the passage in the copy you are actually reading. Record the title or source identifier, edition information if available and a locator that lets you return to the passage. Write the relationship in your own words without quotation marks. Does the passage describe an association, an event sequence, a proposed mechanism, a recommendation or an intervention comparison? More than one can appear in a paragraph, and each should retain its own evidentiary basis.

OpenStax Psychology 2e's Analyzing Findings section, read October 29, 2026, distinguishes observed association from cause and effect and discusses possible third-variable explanations and experimental assignment. That introductory vocabulary is useful for separating reading questions. This guide adopts none of its illustrative studies or examples and does not treat the section as a complete manual for causal inference. The business-reading cases below are newly authored and support only their stated finite comparisons.

Do not begin by telling an assistant to prove the author right or wrong. Ask it first to preserve the source's relationship language and identify what information is supplied about the comparison. If the passage says associated with, retain that qualification. If it explicitly claims caused, record that claim faithfully and then examine its support separately. Careful attribution does not require silently weakening an author's actual assertion or strengthening an author's qualified observation.

A change over time is not yet an effect of one action

Fictional Case A supplies a note about an invented team's output: ten completed items in an earlier period and fifteen in a later period. A new checklist was introduced between those periods. The absolute difference is five items; relative to ten, the change is one half, or fifty percent. The supplied note establishes these arithmetic relationships and the stated sequence. It does not identify which part of the change, if any, was produced by the checklist.

A proposed paraphrase saying the checklist caused five additional completions would add a causal allocation absent from the note. Other conditions are unknown: the work could differ, staffing could change, the periods could have different lengths or counting rules could shift. These are questions to investigate, not findings that any particular alternative actually occurred. The appropriate reading note preserves the increase and the timing while leaving causal attribution unresolved under the supplied information.

An AI prompt can ask for two sentences: one describing what the fictional record states and another naming the unanswered comparison question. The first might describe the ten-to-fifteen increase after the checklist's introduction. The second would ask what the later period would have looked like under a suitable comparison without that change. This is an authored instruction pattern, not a tested guarantee of model behavior or a recommendation to run an actual workplace intervention.

Make the comparison population explicit

Fictional Case B supplies two invented groups. Among twenty checklist users, twelve meet a declared completion condition; among twenty nonusers, eight meet it. The proportions are three fifths and two fifths. The difference is one fifth, or twenty percentage points, while their ratio is three halves. Those descriptive comparisons use the supplied group counts. They do not identify the effect of assigning the checklist to the same kind of person under otherwise comparable conditions.

The fixture does not say how anyone entered either group. People may have chosen their group, been assigned under an unknown rule or differed before the recorded period. Do not invent random assignment because the group sizes happen to match. Likewise, do not diagnose self-selection merely because assignment is unreported. Write assignment method unknown. Equal denominators simplify a displayed comparison but do not establish comparability, independence, measurement quality or an adequate design.

Preserve both numerators and denominators when shortening a note. A statement that twelve users succeeded and eight nonusers succeeded conceals the number in each group. A statement that users were fifty percent higher is ambiguous unless it identifies the measured proportion and the reference group. The difference of twenty percentage points and the relative ratio of one and a half describe the same authored counts differently; neither arithmetic form upgrades them into a causal effect.

Consider a third variable without pretending to discover one

Fictional Case C supplies two invented task categories, simple and complex. Within simple tasks, checklist users complete eight of ten and nonusers complete eight of ten. Within complex tasks, each group completes two of ten. The completion proportions are therefore equal within each category: four fifths for simple tasks and one fifth for complex tasks. These equalities are properties of the authored table, not a finding about an actual checklist or workforce.

Now construct unequal mixes from those same category rates. The user group has twenty simple tasks and ten complex tasks, with sixteen plus two completions: eighteen of thirty, or three fifths. The nonuser group has ten simple and twenty complex tasks, with eight plus four completions: twelve of thirty, or two fifths. The pooled difference is twenty percentage points despite equal category proportions in this deliberately constructed comparison. The category mix changes the pooled description.

This example demonstrates why a summary should ask which populations are being combined. It does not prove that task complexity confounds any real study, that adjustment for one recorded variable establishes causation or that equal within-category proportions imply a general absence of effect. A real analysis needs justified variables, source definitions and a suitable design. For reading purposes, the immediate action is to keep the grouping information visible and identify what the passage actually reports.

Separate a mechanism proposal from evidence that it occurred

Fictional Case D supplies a proposed chain: a checklist makes a requirement more visible, a person notices it, and fewer items need rework. The supplied record marks the checklist as introduced and records a later rework count. It leaves noticing and the connection between noticing and rework unobserved. There are three named links in the proposed explanation, but naming them does not supply observations for every link or identify a total causal effect.

A mechanism can be a useful hypothesis to understand an author's reasoning. Keep its status as proposed unless the passage supplies relevant support. Do not insert an employee quotation, a recorded attention measure or a verified behavioral change merely because it would make the story more coherent. A plausible account should help formulate the next evidence question. It should not be presented as a completed inspection of a process that neither reader nor assistant observed.

Ask an assistant to distinguish the intervention description, intermediate explanation and outcome measure in separate sentences. Then inspect whether it has changed a possibility into an occurrence. Words such as may help someone notice differ from did make everyone notice. The distinction applies even if the proposed mechanism seems ordinary. The fixture contains no actual reader, employee, intervention execution, observation, client result or operational authorization.

Chronology can rule out a simple story without proving another

Fictional Case E supplies three authored dates: a performance increase is recorded on June 10, 2030; a checklist is introduced on June 12; a reader writes a note on June 15. The increase date precedes introduction by two days. The note date is three days after introduction and five days after the recorded increase. These calendar differences are exact for the supplied fictional dates. They are not dates from a real business, study or book passage.

If a proposed note says that the June 12 introduction produced the already recorded June 10 increase, the described chronology does not support that simple forward sequence. A reader should flag the mismatch and return to the source. Perhaps a date or event label was misunderstood, but the fixture does not establish which explanation is correct. Do not repair the dates, invent an earlier pilot or conclude that a different cause has been found.

A publication date also differs from an event date, data-collection period or intervention date. Keeping those fields separate prevents a summary from using a later source as though it were a later event. A chronology check can identify a specific inconsistency in an explanation while leaving the broader causal question unresolved. That limited conclusion is useful because it tells the reader exactly what to recheck rather than replacing one unsupported story with another.

Missing outcomes are not unsuccessful outcomes

Fictional Case F supplies four labeled cases. Their outcomes are success, failure, success and unknown. Three outcomes are available and two of those are successes, giving two thirds among resolved cases. Availability is three fourths of the four supplied cases. The full four-case success proportion remains unresolved. If the unknown outcome were success, it would be three fourths; if failure, it would be one half. Those are scenarios, not probabilities or predictions.

A summary that calls the unresolved record a failure chooses a missing-data rule without stating it. A summary that drops the record and reports two thirds for all cases changes the denominator. Neither operation identifies an effect of an intervention, even if the outcomes were complete. Keep outcome availability and the descriptive outcome proportion separate. The missingness could have many explanations, but this authored record supplies no basis to identify one.

When requesting AI assistance, ask it to retain unknown values explicitly and label scenario calculations. Do not let a neatly completed table conceal a missing source field. If the actual passage gives a reason for exclusion, preserve that reason and its scope. If it does not, record what is absent. An unknown outcome is a limit on the available comparison; it is not evidence that the source withheld a result or that a study was conducted improperly.

Keep recommendations separate from causal findings

An author can recommend an action for several reasons: a proposed mechanism, a practical constraint, a descriptive pattern or an explicitly reported intervention result. A reading note should preserve the reason the author gives. A recommendation is not itself evidence that the action has been tested, and an observed association does not become an implementation instruction simply because a reader wants something actionable. Identify the bridge between the evidence and the proposed use.

In the fictional checklist examples, a reader might draft a question about trying a clearer reminder in a future bounded exercise. That proposed application is separate from claiming the checklist caused the earlier increase. It also remains separate from permission to change an actual process, collect information about people or represent an author's endorsement. This guide authorizes no operational action and reports no actual trial, training result, productivity improvement or client experience.

A useful application note states what you want to understand, which part comes from the passage and which part you are proposing. If an evidence gap matters to the proposed application, retain it near the proposal. Do not hide it in a general disclaimer at the bottom of a report. The reader reviewing the action should be able to see the comparison limitations before deciding whether further investigation is appropriate.

Use alternative explanations as questions

Asking about alternatives can improve a reading discussion, but a list of possibilities is not evidence that any of them occurred. A model may generate familiar explanations such as changing demand, different task mix, learning over time or altered selection. Treat these as candidate questions only. Connect each question to a specific missing field or comparison in the supplied passage rather than presenting a long generic list as an investigation result.

For Case A, a concrete question is whether the two periods used the same duration and completion definition. For Case B, ask how the groups were formed. For Case C, retain the category counts before pooling. For Case D, ask which intermediate links were observed. These questions arise from different features of the authored cases. They should not be collapsed into a single verdict that every causal claim is false or that every descriptive comparison is useless.

A reader can understand an argument while reserving judgment about its support. Record the author's claim accurately, the evidence the passage presents and the unresolved connection. This allows a later source review to address a defined question. It also avoids a common failure in critical reading: replacing unearned certainty with an equally unearned rejection. The worksheet supplies no professional assessment of an actual author, publication or research design.

Inspect the assistant's verbs and referents

After an assistant drafts a summary, compare its verbs with your supplied note. Watch for movement from followed to produced, associated with to improved, or proposed to demonstrated. A change can occur in a heading, caption or short takeaway even when the main paragraph remains qualified. Inspect the complete summary that will be reused, not only the sentence that contains the numeric calculation.

Check the subject and population too. A statement about twenty supplied users should not become a statement about all businesses, every reader or a named client. A period-specific count should not become a universal response to an action. A source's reported explanation should not become the assistant's independently verified finding. Preserve who made the claim, which group it concerns and what the reader actually inspected.

These checks support traceability rather than semantic certification. The local worksheet can verify arithmetic and literal fields, but it cannot certify the meaning of an unseen passage or judge the truth of a real causal claim. A human reader still needs to inspect relevant source context. No observed AI output is included here, and the proposed instructions are teaching examples rather than a measured model-evaluation protocol.

Return to the supporting source with a narrow question

If a passage cites a study, the citation is a lead to another source. Record enough information to locate it, and distinguish the book's description from the source material you actually obtain. An abstract, a full paper, a supplement and a later correction can contain different information. Do not claim to have reviewed all of them because one link was opened or because a model supplied a fluent account of the study.

Formulate a question before following the lead. You might need the assignment method, the definition of the outcome, the period covered or the treatment of unavailable cases. That question helps determine what material must be inspected. The current guide performs no actual study search or research appraisal, and it does not invent the contents of any cited paper. Its point is to keep the route from claim to support visible in the reading note.

When information changes, revise the affected relationship rather than rewriting the entire record as though the evidence had always been complete. Preserve the earlier uncertainty and identify what the newly inspected source resolves. An improved source note may sharpen a question without resolving causation. The useful result is a reading record that distinguishes description, explanation, evidence and proposed application clearly enough for another reader to examine.

Questions about causal language in AI reading notes

Does an increase after an action prove that the action worked?

The sequence alone does not allocate the change to that action. Case A supplies an increase from ten to fifteen and an introduction between periods, but leaves the relevant comparison unresolved. Preserve the five-item and fifty-percent descriptive changes without calling them measured causal effects. Ask what evidence the passage offers about otherwise comparable conditions and the source's outcome definition.

Are equal group sizes enough for a causal comparison?

No. Case B has twenty records in each group, which makes its descriptive fractions straightforward, but the assignment method is unknown. Equal sizes do not establish comparable starting conditions or random assignment. Keep the twelve-of-twenty and eight-of-twenty counts visible and describe the twenty-percentage-point difference as the supplied comparison, without promoting it into an intervention effect.

Does finding a possible third variable disprove the author?

A possible alternative identifies a question, not a discovered cause or a completed rebuttal. Case C is a deliberately constructed table showing how group mix can change pooled descriptions. It does not establish that a particular variable explains an actual study. Read the author's stated claim faithfully, inspect relevant support and retain unresolved alternatives as questions rather than findings.

Can a plausible mechanism fill a missing evidence link?

A proposed mechanism can organize an explanation and suggest what to examine, but it does not supply observations. Case D leaves intermediate noticing unobserved. An assistant should not invent a quotation, attention measure or verified behavioral change to complete that chain. Keep proposed links separate from supplied outcomes, and identify the evidence question attached to each missing connection.

How should unavailable outcomes appear in a summary?

Preserve them as unknown and distinguish availability from the descriptive proportion among resolved cases. Case F has three of four outcomes available and two of those three successful. Its full proportion is unresolved, with hypothetical completions producing one half or three fourths. Neither the resolved-case calculation nor either scenario establishes a causal effect or an actual outcome for the missing record.

Does this workflow evaluate a book or an AI model?

It provides original examples for preparing and inspecting reading notes. No published business-book passage, manuscript, actual study or observed model response was evaluated. Any application later proposed by a reader remains separate from the source claim and from permission to act. The checks here concern authored arithmetic and fields; they establish no professional review, endorsement, learning gain or operational result.
