Read a Book Equation Before Asking AI for a Business Explanation

Before asking AI to explain an equation from a business book, copy its structure, definitions and units. A correct calculation answers a mathematical question; applying it to a real business also requires evidence for the inputs and assumptions.

This guide uses six original fictional cases. They are not excerpts from Connor Robertson’s books, observed AI responses or client results. The downloadable practice CSV preserves the original inputs; the blank reading record helps you review your own permitted source material.

Five-step reading record · Six worked cases · Review an AI explanation

Build a five-step reading record

  1. Locate the source. Record the book, edition and page or other locator. Keep the nearby definition, caption and footnote when they control the equation. Mark referenced material you have not read as unprovided.
  2. Transcribe before simplifying. Preserve parentheses, fraction bars, exponents and subscripts. Write an ambiguous layout as a question. Do not let an assistant choose the most convenient interpretation.
  3. Define each input. Record the symbol, its local meaning, value and unit. Distinguish an observed input, an authored example, an assumption and an unknown. A familiar letter does not establish its meaning.
  4. Show the calculation. Write the substitution, unit conversions and intermediate steps. Preserve restrictions such as a nonzero denominator. Keep exact fractions separate from rounded displays.
  5. Bound the conclusion. State what the expression calculates under the supplied inputs. List the missing information needed before making a forecast or operating decision.

Six worked cases: what changes the answer?

The cases below preserve the original fictional data. Read the calculation and the interpretation together. A numerical difference alone does not establish a real mistake, a business trend or an author’s intention.

Ask for an explanation you can check

Supply only material you are permitted to share. Include the equation and the relevant definitions rather than asking an assistant to reconstruct a passage from its title. Use this request with your completed reading record:

Transcribe the supplied equation before interpreting it. Define each symbol from the supplied material, preserving subscripts, units and restrictions. Show the substitution and arithmetic. Keep unknown inputs unknown. Separate the calculated result from any proposed business application. List ambiguous notation or missing source material as questions.

This is a proposed prompt, not evidence that any model will follow it. Compare the response against the record. In case A, an answer of 14 has changed the grouping. In case C, an answer of 3,600 has used incompatible time units. In case F, a single numerical answer needs an explicitly added assumption.

Unknown, undefined and rounded are different states

An unknown input means information is missing. An undefined expression can have fully supplied inputs, as with division by zero. A rounded number is available but may differ from the source’s exact value. Keep these states visible rather than replacing all three with zero, a blank or a guessed number.

Domain restrictions also survive simplification. A shorter formula should not silently gain inputs excluded by the original operations. For a separate worked explanation of that issue, use the function composition guide.

What would justify a business conclusion?

First identify whether the source is defining a quantity, presenting a hypothetical example or making a claim about observed results. Then ask for the evidence connecting its inputs to the proposed use. Compatible units and correct arithmetic do not establish that a rate is measured, stays constant or applies to another team.

For case C, the record supports 60 invented items under a stated constant-rate assumption. It supplies nothing about interruptions, setup time or changing work mix. A staffing recommendation would need additional evidence. Keep that open question with the calculation rather than converting the example into an expected outcome.
