Read the transition packet before drawing the conclusion

An ownership transition can produce three different questions: how much cash a sale releases, whether a proposed exchange fits a timeline, and who can make a decision for someone else. A packet that answers one question does not automatically answer the others. This exercise gives each question a separate fictional record so you can see where the evidence stops.

These are original website exercises, not verified book chapters, manuscript quotations or actual transactions. E01, X01 and L01 are independent scenarios. No person owns all three fictional situations, and the numbers are not an investment forecast. Read the supplied record, write a short finding in the CSV, and compare your reasoning with the supported interpretation.

A useful finding identifies both the calculation you can make and the decision you cannot yet make. “There is cash after a payoff” and “there is no taxable gain” are different statements. “A person is listed as a contact” and “that person can sign” are different statements. Preserve those distinctions when handing a packet to a reviewer.

E01: Cash released by a sale is not the same as taxable gain

The fictional cash-sale worksheet has a $900,000 gross price, $45,000 of assumed selling costs and a $420,000 loan-principal payoff. For this simplified exercise, all $900,000 is cash consideration; there is no seller note, buyer assumption of debt, other consideration or additional closing adjustment. The stated payoff is principal only. These are supplied assumptions, not a description of typical closing costs.

The cash arithmetic is $900,000 minus $45,000 minus $420,000, leaving $435,000 before income taxes and any omitted adjustments. A separate price-less-assumed-selling-cost subtotal is $855,000. The $435,000 cash figure subtracts the debt payoff; it does not calculate tax basis or establish the character of a gain.

The packet has no acquisition-basis schedule, improvement records, depreciation history or allocation among assets. Therefore it cannot produce a supported taxable-gain figure or an after-tax distribution. Do not substitute the $420,000 debt balance for adjusted basis. Even if debt and basis happen to match in a particular transaction, that relationship must come from records rather than an assumption.

IRS Publication 544 (2025) explains gain in relation to amount realized and adjusted basis, including basis adjustments and relevant liabilities. It is the source for the distinction, not an endorsement of this fictional worksheet. A complete review would reconcile the actual closing statement and basis records before calculating reportable gain, recapture, exclusions or applicable tax.

X01: Check both exchange clocks and the missing conditions

The separate fictional deferred-exchange timeline supplies a relinquished-property transfer date of November 20, 2026. It also supplies April 15, 2027 as the assumed applicable return due date, with no extension. That due date is an exercise input, not a determination of a real taxpayer’s filing deadline. The packet contains no identification notice, receipt evidence, exchange agreement or replacement-property records.

Counting from the supplied transfer date gives January 4, 2027 at 45 days and May 19, 2027 at 180 days. Comparing the assumed return due date with May 19 yields April 15 as the earlier receipt-date limit in this exercise. Writing only “180 days” on the worksheet would miss that earlier assumed date. An actual extension or different applicable deadline would require a new review.

The IRS Form 8824 instructions (2026) explain that the replacement property must be identified within 45 days, and received by the earlier of 180 days or the applicable return due date, including extensions. They also limit section 1031 treatment to qualifying real property held for business or investment, excluding property held primarily for sale. Timing alone does not establish qualification.

This packet therefore supports a date comparison only. It does not prove a valid identification, timely receipt, eligible property or a completed exchange. It does not establish that receiving sale cash and later buying property creates an exchange. Before any real transfer, the transaction structure and documents need review; this worksheet cannot create or repair an exchange arrangement.

L01: A family contact is not an authority document

The third invented packet contains an account-contact list naming “Family Contact A” and a folder index labeled “future planning.” There is no power of attorney, trust instrument, appointment record or account-specific authorization in the packet. A handwritten note says the contact knows the owner’s preferences. Nothing in the supplied records says the contact accepted a formal role.

The supported conclusion is narrow: a contact and a planning-folder label exist, but the packet does not establish signing or decision-making authority. This is not a finding that no authority exists anywhere. The missing document may exist elsewhere; its location, current status and scope need to be checked. Do not treat a blank evidence field as proof of either permission or prohibition.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that a power of attorney is a legal document through which someone can act on another person’s behalf. Its guidance also discusses the risks of that authority. In this exercise, a family relationship and knowledge of preferences are not a substitute for reviewing the relevant document and its scope.

A useful follow-up request identifies the exact decision under review, the document said to authorize it, and who will assess that document. Do not put account numbers, passwords, signatures or personal records into this public teaching CSV. The CSV is a fictional reading exercise, not a place to collect a family’s financial or estate-planning documents.

Complete your own review without inventing missing outcomes

Use the empty observed_review column to record what you concluded from the fictional packet. For E01, show the cash arithmetic and name the missing basis records. For X01, show both date calculations and the earlier-date comparison, then identify the absent transaction evidence. For L01, distinguish a contact record from authority evidence without asserting that a missing document does not exist.

The three blanks are intentional. They do not represent zero tax, a failed exchange or a completed authority review. Each row is marked fictional. The supplied interpretations are teaching answers under stated assumptions, not findings about a real sale or family. Keep any proposed request separate from confirmation that the record has been received and reviewed.

Three fictional transition record packets

E01: Sale-cash worksheet

Supplied record
Fictional cash price $900,000; assumed selling costs $45,000; principal payoff $420,000; no other consideration or adjustments. Basis and depreciation records absent.
Supported interpretation
Cash before tax and omitted adjustments is $435,000; price less assumed selling costs is $855,000. Taxable gain and after-tax cash are not established.
Review question
Which actual closing and adjusted-basis records are missing before a gain calculation can be reviewed?

X01: Exchange timeline only

Supplied record
Independent fictional transfer November 20, 2026; assumed applicable return due date April 15, 2027 with no extension. No identification, receipt or exchange documents supplied.
Supported interpretation
45 days gives January 4, 2027; 180 days gives May 19, 2027. April 15 is the earlier assumed receipt limit. No qualification or completed exchange is established.
Review question
Which identification, receipt, property-use and transaction-structure records are absent?

L01: Family-contact record

Supplied record
Family Contact A appears on a contact list; a future-planning folder index and preference note exist. No authority instrument or role acceptance is supplied.
Supported interpretation
The packet establishes a contact only; authority is unverified, not disproved. Missing evidence is not permission to act.
Review question
Which exact decision, authority document and scope review would be needed before relying on someone else’s signature?

Primary sources and scope

  • IRS Instructions for Form 8824 (2026): Qualifying real-property scope and deferred-exchange identification and receipt timing.
